How the estimate works

The calculator uses the Epley formula, the same one marble uses for the estimated 1RM in Progress:

1RM = weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30)

Each rep adds a thirtieth of the weight. The rep tables run the formula backwards, weight = 1RM ÷ (1 + reps ÷ 30), to suggest a load for a target rep count.

Epley and Brzycki compared

Brzycki is the other common formula: 1RM = weight × 36 ÷ (37 − reps). The two give exactly the same answer at 10 reps. Below 10 reps Epley reads a little higher, and above 10 Brzycki does.

What the estimate leaves out

  • Effort. The formula assumes a hard set. Five reps that stopped well short of failure will read low. marble deliberately doesn’t adjust its estimate for RPE, so a set you never rated isn’t quietly inflated.
  • High reps. Past about 12 reps, endurance decides the set more than strength. marble leaves those sets out of its estimates instead of forcing a number, and this calculator stops at 12.
  • Singles. Applied as written, Epley adds a thirtieth even to one rep, so 300 × 1 reads 310. Some calculators return a single unchanged; this one and marble keep every rep count on the same scale.
  • The lift itself. The same formula is used for every exercise. Machines, cables and bodyweight movements often estimate less reliably than squats, presses and deadlifts.

Track it without the math

In marble, every set you log or paste feeds Progress. For each exercise it charts an estimated 1RM from your best set each day, keeps your all-time best and the date you set it, and says whether the lift is progressing or holding. Pair it with your bodyweight and you also get a DOTS line for relative strength. Not sure how to write a set down? See workout notation explained.

Questions

What is a one-rep max?

Your one-rep max (1RM) is the most weight you can lift for a single full repetition of an exercise with good form. An estimated 1RM (e1RM) predicts it from a lighter set you can do for several reps.

How do you calculate a one-rep max?

With the Epley formula, multiply the weight by one plus the reps divided by 30: 1RM = weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30). For example, 100 kg for 5 reps estimates 116.7 kg.

How accurate is a 1RM calculator?

Estimates are closest for sets of about 1 to 10 reps taken near failure. They drift as reps climb, and they read low when a set stopped well short of failure. Treat the number as a trend to track, not a promise of what you can lift today.

Which 1RM formula is best?

No formula fits everyone. Epley and Brzycki are the most common, and they give exactly the same estimate at 10 reps. Below 10 reps Epley reads slightly higher; above 10, Brzycki does. Using one formula consistently matters more than which one you pick.

Why does the calculator stop at 12 reps?

Rep-based formulas get unreliable past roughly 10 to 12 reps, because endurance starts to decide the set more than strength. marble leaves sets above 12 reps out of its estimates rather than forcing a number.

Does marble calculate my one-rep max automatically?

Yes. Progress charts an estimated 1RM for each exercise from your best set each day, using the Epley formula and sets of 12 reps or fewer, and tells you whether the lift is progressing or holding. It runs on your iPhone, with no account.
