How to track sprint times to the tenth of a second

In marble, a sprint exercise holds plans with a distance, a rep count and a target time. Time each rep with the built-in stopwatch or type it in, for example 14.8, and marble marks every rep as hit or missed and keeps your fastest time for each distance.

Set up a sprint plan

  1. Open the Exercise Library. Tap Manage in the exercise picker, or go to Settings, then Data & Backups, then Exercise Library. Add or edit an exercise.
  2. Choose the Sprint tracking type. Every rep then needs a distance and a time.
  3. Add a plan. Set the distance (for example 100 m), the number of reps (1 to 50), and either one target time (“14.5 s or faster”) or a range such as 19–21.5 s. Decimal seconds are fine.
  4. Add more plans if you need them. One exercise can hold several, such as a 60 m speed plan and a 150 m tempo plan.
  5. Set the default recovery between reps, then save.

Log a session

  1. Tap Add Set on the sprint exercise. marble picks your most recent plan, locks the distance and starts at Rep 1 of 4. Use the Plan menu to switch plans.
  2. Start the stopwatch before the rep and stop it after, or type the time, such as 14.8. Add RPE and rest if you like.
  3. Save. marble checks the time against the target, and the rest timer starts before the next rep.
  4. On the last rep, tap Save Final Rep. You’ll see how many reps hit the target, your best and average times, and the result for every rep.

Reading your results

  • Hit, missed or not scored. Each rep in Log shows its time and target with a check or a cross. A rep with no time, or run at a different distance, is left unscored. The result is always written out, never shown by colour alone.
  • Fastest times. A rep that beats every earlier time for the same exercise and distance gets a Fastest badge.
  • Trends. In Progress, open Details to chart your best times at your most-logged distance, along with a weekly goal hit rate.
  • A nudge to go faster. Hit the target on at least 80% of scored reps in each of your last two sessions, and marble suggests a target 0.2 s faster.

Every rep also stays a normal entry in your journal, so your sprints sit next to your lifting.

Writing sprints as text

In the Add tab, Sprints 4x20m becomes four 20 m reps, and 4x20meter accelerations at 85-90% becomes four 20 m accelerations. The percentage is ignored, not read as a weight. Times from a typed line are added during review.

Limits worth knowing

  • Times are stored to the tenth of a second, not the hundredth.
  • The stopwatch is a hand-operated timer on the phone. marble doesn’t connect to timing gates or other sensors.
  • There is no Apple Watch app. If you record runs on a watch, the workout summary can come in through Apple Health, but the rep-by-rep sprint times are logged in marble.

Questions

Can marble track 100m sprint times?

Yes. Create a sprint plan with a 100 m distance, a rep count and a target time or range, then time each rep with the stopwatch or type it in to the tenth of a second.

Can one exercise have more than one sprint plan?

Yes. For example, a 60 m speed plan and a 150 m tempo plan can live on the same sprint exercise. You switch between them from the Plan menu.

Does marble record hundredths of a second?

No. Sprint times are stored in tenths of a second.

Where do I see my sprint progress?

Each rep in Log shows whether it hit the target, the fastest reps get a badge, and Progress Details charts your best times and weekly hit rate.
